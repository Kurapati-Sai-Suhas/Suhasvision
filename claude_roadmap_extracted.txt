SECTION 2  
  Research Gaps Your Project Fills
Your Cricket Stance Analyzer fills 8 distinct gaps that no existing paper addresses individually — let alone as a unified system. These gaps form the core contribution claims of your IEEE paper.
Gap 1 — Stance Execution Quality vs. Shot Type Classification
This is your strongest and most defensible gap. Every existing paper answers 'what shot?'. You answer 'how well?'
All 15+ cricket ML papers surveyed classify which shot is being played (cover drive, pull, sweep, etc.). None measure the quality of the player's stance and body positioning during execution. Your system is the first to score six simultaneous biomechanical dimensions — Head Position, Foot Placement, Knee Bend, Bat Angle, Hip Rotation, and Weight Distribution — with continuous 0–100 scores per dimension, enabling fine-grained coaching at the dimension level.

Gap 2 — First Vision LLM Applied to Cricket Coaching
Gemini 1.5 Flash and Claude Vision have never been applied to cricket technique analysis in any published paper.
The Language and Multimodal Models in Sports survey (2024) explicitly confirms no multimodal LLM has been applied to cricket coaching. Your paper is the first to: (1) use a Vision LLM as a zero-shot cricket biomechanics evaluator, (2) engineer a structured prompt that extracts JSON-formatted scores across 6 dimensions, and (3) compare the LLM's scores against human coach ground-truth labels. This is a research first.
Gap 3 — No Multi-Dimensional Biomechanical Scoring Framework
Even papers that use MediaPipe (Kannan et al., 2023; Maduwantha et al., 2024) use pose data only for shot classification. No paper defines and implements a multi-dimensional scoring rubric with biomechanical rules for cricket batting. Your six-dimension framework with defined ideal angular ranges (e.g., front knee 150–165 degrees, stance width 80–120% shoulder width) is a novel research contribution.
Gap 4 — Automated Drill Prescription Linked to Biomechanical Deficits
Papers detect faults. Yours prescribes solutions. No existing paper closes this loop.
Existing systems identify that a player's knee bend is poor — but stop there. Your system maps each detected dimension deficit to a targeted drill prescription (title, description, difficulty, duration, YouTube search term). The Tissera et al. (2024) coaching survey establishes that drill prescription is exactly what coaches want from technology. You are the first to automate this mapping.
Gap 5 — Progressive Hybrid LLM → Custom Model Architecture
No prior work proposes or evaluates a progressive architecture that begins with a zero-shot Vision LLM (Phase 1) and transitions to a custom MediaPipe-based neural network (Phase 2+) as training data grows — with both backends sharing the same API interface. This design enables immediate deployment before labelled data is available, and provides a natural experimental comparison for the paper (LLM vs rule-based vs custom NN).
Gap 6 — Browser-Accessible Web Platform for Amateur and Grassroots Players
Maduwantha et al. (2024) defines the accessibility problem. Your project is the deployed solution.
Existing AI cricket platforms (CricVision, Advanced Impactor, CrickCoach AI) are priced for academies and professional teams. No published research has deployed a self-serve, browser-accessible cricket coaching platform that amateurs can use with a smartphone video. Your deployment on Railway/Render with no app installation required directly addresses the accessibility gap Maduwantha et al. formally identified.
Gap 7 — Stance-Context Conditioning Before Analysis
No prior paper conditions the analysis model on player-declared parameters before running inference. Your system collects batting hand, shot type, grip type, stance width preference, player experience level, and pitch conditions — then conditions the LLM prompt and model on these parameters. This produces contextually appropriate feedback (a beginner defensive stance is scored differently from a professional offensive one), which is novel in the sports AI literature.
Gap 8 — Longitudinal Progress Tracking for Amateur Players
Every existing tool produces a one-off analysis report. Your session-based database schema enables longitudinal stance improvement tracking — comparing a player's scores over time, identifying which dimensions improved and which regressed. The proposed coach dashboard with multi-player comparison adds a second novel application layer beyond self-coaching.

Critical Tips for IEEE Access Acceptance
Write your cover letter carefully — explicitly list all 8 contributions and explain why IEEE Access (not a conference) is the right venue. Cover letters are read first.
Ensure reproducibility — link to your GitHub repo, Zenodo dataset DOI, and HuggingFace model weights in the Data Availability Statement. IEEE Access specifically looks for this.
Submit a supplementary video — a 60-second screen recording of the app analysing a cricket video dramatically increases reviewer confidence in your implementation.
Address the small dataset proactively in the Limitations section. If you only have 400 stances, acknowledge it and explain your augmentation strategy and future collection plan.
Compare to human coach scores — even a 10-player pilot study adds real-world validity that pure ML papers lack.
Put your arXiv preprint live on Day 27 before IEEE submission — this protects your priority date and is considered good research practice.
Check if your institution (likely a technical college or IIT/NIT/BITS) has an IEEE Transformative Agreement — if so, the Article Processing Charge is waived. Check with your library.
